01 / Curiositya&b

There’s a person
on the other side
of the product.

That’s where I like to begin.

For 15 years, I’ve worked on systems people depend on and rarely see.

At PerfectServe, that meant helping a nurse reach a doctor at 2 a.m. At BlueCross BlueShield of Tennessee, it meant helping a member know where to go. At TheraMatch, it meant helping someone find the right therapist.

Every new workflow still piques my curiosity about who it serves and what it could become. And most importantly, how we can make it simpler for people to actually get care.

02 / Product judgmentMake the first use count

The first hour
is part of
the product.

At PerfectServe, new providers went from waiting five to seven days to starting in under an hour.

I started in hospital implementations and grew into product leadership. I helped build the product and admin systems that made that change possible.

What does each new customer need before they can benefit from what they bought? The answer can change the product, how we introduce it, or the support around it. I love figuring that out with the people who know the job.

03 / Make it usefulStart small. Learn from use.

Give people
something useful.
Keep learning.

At One to One Health, clinicians needed value while we built the larger product suite. We started with their notes.

The company’s first AI product cut charting time from 22 to 17.5 minutes per encounter, with clinician and patient satisfaction maintained or improved.

That gave staff time back before the wider plan was complete. It’s how I like to build: keep the larger aim in view, and find a useful place to start.
