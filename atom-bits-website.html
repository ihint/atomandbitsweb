Ian Harman · Behavioral health founder & advisor

I help behavioral health companies get more people into care, and get paid for it.

At One to One Health, we could hire clinicians and still wait for patients to get through eligibility and onboarding. More staff could not, by itself, get people into care. Since then, I’ve worked on every step between someone asking for help and a practice getting paid for the visit: matching, eligibility, claims, and payment.

I’m Ian Harman. I’m building the administrative and financial infrastructure for behavioral health in the United States, and I work alongside the practices, founders, and operators who deliver that care.

One to One Health

Hiring clinicians was only part of getting people into care.

When I joined One to One Health, patients valued the care and clinicians enjoyed delivering it. But each new client asked more of a team already relying on manual processes, spreadsheets, and knowledge held by a few people.

Eligibility and onboarding belonged in our product plan alongside clinical care. I set that direction for the full product suite and hired the founding engineers. Building that suite would take time, and clinical and operations staff needed something useful before it could deliver.

So I chose clinical documentation as a place to start. I designed and developed the initial AI infrastructure to prepare clinical notes for provider review. Charting time fell from 22 to 17.5 minutes across all encounters, which gave clinicians back 4.5 minutes on an average encounter, while clinician and patient satisfaction were maintained or improved.

How I work

My favorite moment is a question that changes the plan.

At TPN, we were discussing invoicing payers directly, and I asked, “What exactly are we trying to achieve here?” Were we trying to improve throughput? Invoicing? Payouts? Working through the answer together turned a conversation about invoices into a plan for a claims filing and revenue capture platform. I love that turn, when someone supplies a missing detail and a team suddenly sees a more useful thing to build.

Building is part of the fun, too. I built TheraMatch’s app in Elixir and Phoenix, from intake to matching logic, and today I’m building Claims Native. Before we meet, I do my research, and I’ll bring a point of view while leaving room for your team to change my mind.

Home is Chattanooga, Tennessee, where my wife and I are raising three children alongside three dogs and ten chickens, and where I serve on the board of The Mission Chattanooga. I most enjoy working with curious, kind people who can disagree well and still find joy in reaching an answer we can put to use.
