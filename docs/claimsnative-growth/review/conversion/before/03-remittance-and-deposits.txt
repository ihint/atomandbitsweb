    - paragraph: Practice billing guide · Reviewed October 15, 2026
    - heading "Does a remittance mean the money reached your bank?" [level=1]
    - paragraph: No. A remittance explains a payer’s decision about claims. It does not, by itself, prove that the practice received money in its bank account. Check the remittance and the bank record together.
    - heading "Match the explanation to the money" [level=2]
    - paragraph:
      - text: "The payer may send the electronic remittance advice and the funds transfer through different channels. CMS describes reassociation: matching the payment to the remittance using the matching trace information. The files can arrive at different times. See"
      - link "CMS’s payment and remittance guide":
        - /url: https://www.cms.gov/files/document/eft-and-era-payment-remittance-reassociation-basics.pdf
      - text: .
    - paragraph: For a useful comparison, keep the payer, payment amount, payment date and trace information together in your secure billing records. Check the bank’s posted record. A pending transfer, a clearinghouse acknowledgment and an explanation of benefits each prove a different thing.
    - heading "When the amounts differ" [level=2]
    - paragraph: First check whether the bank payment covers more than one remittance or claim. Then review the payer’s adjustments and any offsets. A difference may need an explanation before anyone can decide whether the payer owes more or a patient owes anything. Do not turn an unexplained difference into a patient balance.
    - paragraph: Keep an unmatched payment open with a named next action. Ask your billing service to show which amounts match, which need review and who owns the follow-up. Keep patient records, bank details and payment trace numbers out of inquiry forms and email.
    - heading "Rejection, denial and receipt are different" [level=2]
    - paragraph:
      - text: A claim acknowledgment can report acceptance or rejection. That is not a payment. A later remittance explains the payer’s adjudication, including adjustments or denials.
      - link "Stedi’s workflow guide":
        - /url: https://www.stedi.com/docs/healthcare/claims-processing-workflows-overview
      - text: shows those stages. Follow the actual response and the payer’s rules before choosing a correction or appeal.
    - heading "What to check with Claims Native" [level=2]
    - paragraph: Ask us how your practice will supply remittances and bank evidence, who reviews differences and where results will appear in your EHR. We confirm the connection approach during onboarding. A working remittance import does not establish a live bank connection or verified deposits; those depend on the practice’s payment setup and available records.
