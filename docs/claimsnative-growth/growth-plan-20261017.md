## Deal snapshot

The offer is billing support for independent medical practices that keep their EHR, with the approach and readiness confirmed in onboarding. Public copy remains nationwide and across specialties, for professional claims. The proposed first acquisition cohort is owner-led practices with a specific billing problem and a workflow we can validate. That cohort is a test, not a claim that one specialty or EHR is already supported.

There is no CRM-backed pipeline, verified channel volume, conversion baseline, acquisition cost, delivery capacity or settled service cash in this source packet. The website has an unsent email composer, a separate app signup and a disabled event collector. Daniel is the user-selected recipient; an email click is not an inbox receipt. The strategy uses the supplied offer and repo evidence; it does not invent active deals or accepted owners.

## The conversion choices I would test

Keep the accepted headline, price and main CTA as the baseline. Start with the largest plausible leaks: reaching a real person, seeing how setup works, and finishing the existing signup. Test one change at a time with a dated baseline; do not call a small before/after difference causal.

- **Question path:** a guide reader should be able to ask about their EHR, enrollment or payment problem without estimating hypothetical revenue or opening an account first. The required fields stay name, email and question; other practice facts stay optional.
- **Offer clarity:** show scope, price threshold, quoted launch fee, shadow period and the decision needed to move to live filing. A later scoped fit review may be a better CTA for some traffic, but do not promise a free audit, guaranteed savings or a response deadline without agreeing delivery and terms.
- **Signup confidence:** make cost, commitment, saved progress and next steps clear where the visitor chooses an account. Trace mobile abandonment through verified records, without adding marketing tracking to authenticated healthcare pages.
- **Follow-through:** prepare a reply draft that addresses the actual question and asks for one next practice fact. Trigger only from an actual received inquiry in the approved system; dedupe retries. A nurture sequence needs distinct consent and sending authority.
- **Proof:** demonstrate one real small workflow and its limits. Keep setup evidence, paid service revenue and insurer deposits distinct.

## A weekly decision, not a traffic report

Reuse [the existing private-ledger scorecard](weekly-scorecard.md). Add app-activation counts only from permitted authoritative app records. Keep names, practice details and evidence pointers out of the public repo and marketing analytics. For each source show relevant visits, actual received inquiries, qualified practices, confirmed meetings, agreed pilots, settled service receipts and refunds. Show denominators and missing-source counts. Report both first source and conversion source; the sender's answer stays separate.

Qualification means a professional-claims practice, a stated need in potentially agreed scope, a decision maker or path to one, and a feasible EHR/payer/readiness review. Confirm need, fit and willingness to proceed; an agent cannot declare a live connection or enrolled payer from an EHR name.

Use two weekly questions: Which source produced a qualified practice we can serve? Where did an interested practice stop, and what evidence explains why? Reconcile duplicates before calculating rates. Proposed next cohort target: ten qualified conversations and two paid pilots in 30 days after an authorized launch, subject to capacity. This is a learning target; current baseline and ability to deliver that volume are unknown.

At the current $500 monthly starting fee, three months of gross service receipts would be $1,500 for an eligible practice. That is not contribution profit. A paid-test ceiling must use actual service cost, margin, retention, launch cost, refund risk and available cash. Do not assume a $1,500 acquisition budget or infer payback from a subscription price.
