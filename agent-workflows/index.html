Agent workflow pilot

Give your team less work to repeat.

Your team may spend hours gathering context, comparing records, or preparing drafts before anyone can make a decision. An agent can be useful there, provided reviewing its output takes less effort than preparing it yourself.

I work with you on one focused pilot. We choose a useful task, build around the sources your team trusts, and put the result in front of the people who will use it. Their experience tells us whether it is worth expanding.

What makes a pilot useful

Count the effort of using an agent’s output.

At One to One Health, I developed the initial AI infrastructure for provider note creation. AI prepared a draft for provider review, and charting time fell from 22 to 17.5 minutes across all encounters while clinician and patient satisfaction were maintained or improved.

That experience informs what I look for in a pilot: preparation that software can take on, an output someone can use, and a clear way to judge whether the change helped. Review effort matters. So do the occasions when the information is missing or the system gets something wrong.

Planning a pilot

One task, followed through into use.

01

Choose a task with your team

We follow one repeated task, establish the current effort and exceptions, and agree on a deliverable the team can use. Each pilot has a named owner and a result we can measure.

02

Build for the ordinary day and the awkward one

We connect approved sources and agree on what the system may do. We include human review and a way to recover when inputs are missing, a step fails, or a task is interrupted.

03

Use it and decide what comes next

We run the pilot with its owner and measure output quality, time saved, review effort, and cost. Your team gets an operating guide and evidence for a decision to expand, revise, or stop.

Scope and follow-through

Know what the pilot is there to tell us.

Before we start, we agree on the sources, allowed actions, human decisions, deliverable, measures, price, and delivery responsibility. If the workflow is still unclear, an assessment gives us a way to work that out first.

At the end, we review the pilot in the actual workflow and decide whether to expand, hand over, or stop. Ongoing operation needs its own agreement on support, recovery, access, and cost. Pilot results give us evidence for that decision.
