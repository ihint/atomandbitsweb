Practice margin review

Find the money your practice loses between the visit and the deposit.

Some claims get paid below your contracted rate, and nobody checks. Some denials sit until the appeal deadline passes. Your front desk spends hours on hold, in payer portals, and on prior authorizations that software could prepare. Each of those costs your practice money every month, and few practices can say how much.

In two weeks, I go through twelve months of your own claims and payment data, talk with the people who run your front desk and billing, and put a dollar figure on each loss. You leave with a ranked plan, the first fix underway, and a clear view of where AI can take hours off your staff and where it can’t.

Why now

Insurers automated their side of the claim. Most practices haven’t.

Insurers review claims with software at scale, and each rule they apply shows up in your remittances as a lower payment, a denial, or a request for more records. Most independent practices answer with phone calls, payer portals, and spreadsheets. When a payment comes in short, your team usually posts it and moves on, because nobody has time to compare it with the contract.

At the same time, AI vendors are pitching your practice scribes, phone agents, and prior authorization tools. Some of them can give your staff real hours back. Others add a subscription and another queue to watch. Your own numbers should decide which is which, and that is what this review gives you.

What I look for

Three places money and time leave your practice, each with a dollar figure.

01

Payments below contract

I compare what each payer paid, procedure code by procedure code, with what your contract says it owes. A short payment that repeats becomes a pattern you can appeal, and evidence for your next conversation with that payer.

02

Denials and stuck claims

I rank your denials by reason and dollar value, separate the preventable ones from the rest, and find the claims still inside their appeal deadlines. Claims that have sat past 90 days get the same treatment, payer by payer.

03

Staff hours

I talk with your front desk and billing team about where their day goes: phones, scheduling, eligibility checks, prior authorizations, referrals, and documentation. Then I show which of those hours AI or automation can take now, which tools work with your EHR, and what each one costs.

How we begin

Start with a 30-minute call.

Tell me about your practice: how many providers you have, which payers matter most, and what your billing lead worries about. If a review fits, I’ll send a one-page proposal with the fee and a start date within a day. If it doesn’t fit, I’ll say so.

Before kickoff, we sign a Business Associate Agreement, and your billing lead or clearinghouse pulls twelve months of remittance and claims data, a denial report, A/R aging by payer, and the contracted rates for your top five payers. I ask for claim-level data only, never clinical notes, and files move by secure transfer rather than email.

On day ten, I walk your partners or leadership team through the findings, and we start the first fix together. Filing appeals for you, a certified coding audit, legal advice, and software implementation sit outside the review; if you want help with the fixes, we’ll scope it with the findings in front of us. Claims Native is separate from this review. I recommend whatever fits your practice, and if Claims Native is one of the options, I’ll tell you so in writing.

Common questions

What practice owners ask before the call.

What if we already use a billing company?

The review covers the payments your billing company posts, so you can see what is being recovered and what is not. Many practices share the findings with their billing company and work through the fixes together.

What if we can’t find our payer contracts?

Tell me. Missing contracts are a finding in themselves. I work from the amounts each payer actually paid across your procedure codes while you request current fee schedules.

Is this an AI project?

Sometimes. Some fixes are a rule in your practice management system or a better appeal routine. AI goes in the plan where it saves more staff time than it costs to run and check.

How does the guarantee work?

If the findings add up to less than $45,000 a year in revenue you can recover or staff cost you can remove, each backed by your own data, I waive the second payment of $7,500. The guarantee applies when the data on the checklist arrives within five business days of kickoff.

Does this fit dental, therapy, or behavioral health practices?

Yes, if you bill insurers. Independent medical and specialty groups are my main focus, and the same review works for any practice that gets paid by payers.
