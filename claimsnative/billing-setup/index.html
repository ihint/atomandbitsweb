What needs to be ready before a billing service starts?

A billing service needs an agreed scope, access to the right records, the required payer connections and a checked handoff from your current biller. Opening an account alone does not make a practice ready for live claims.

Prepare a billing question

Review an email draft before you send it. No account or estimate needed. Leave out patient and bank details.

Separate the enrollment steps

Credentialing checks a provider’s qualifications. Joining a payer’s network sets the participation relationship. Transaction enrollment sets up the exchange of electronic records through a clearinghouse. Completing one step does not complete the others. Stedi explains these distinct processes.

Check the payer and the transaction together. The enrollment needed to send a professional claim may differ from the enrollment needed to receive its remittance. Confirm the required authorizations and effective status before depending on that route.

Protect the current payment flow

An electronic remittance advice explains a payer’s claim decisions. Electronic funds transfer moves money. The two need separate checks. Changing where remittances arrive can affect your current biller: Stedi states that its ERA enrollment replaces an existing clearinghouse ERA route. Plan that handoff before requesting a change.

Agree how the billing service will check the old and new routes and follow up on missing responses. Confirm payment instructions with the payer and the practice’s authorized signer. Do not treat a portal connection or a submitted request as completed enrollment.

Agree who does what

  • Name the services, payers and work in scope, and the price.
  • Confirm how signed visits reach the billing service and how results return to your EHR.
  • Sign the service agreement and business associate agreement before sharing patient records.
  • Set the rules for provider questions, corrections and approvals.
  • Compare the work during a shadow period while your current biller keeps working.

How Claims Native starts

We review your practice’s EHR, specialty and payers during onboarding. We confirm the connection approach with you; no direct EHR connection is live yet. We add payers one at a time. Our scope is professional claims, and secondary claims come later. Enrollment status and practice approval remain separate from live filing.

Start with the setup steps. Bring your EHR name and main payers to a conversation, without patient information. If changing systems concerns you, read how to keep your EHR.
