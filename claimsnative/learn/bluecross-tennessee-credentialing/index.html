Billing guide · Tennessee

Getting in network with BlueCross BlueShield of Tennessee.

BlueCross checks your chiropractor, offers a contract for each network and then sets up payments. It aims to finish in 45 business days. An unanswered letter can delay or close an application, so have everything ready before you apply and answer every letter.

Before you apply

BlueCross asks for four things before it will start. Do them first.

  1. An NPI. Your chiropractor’s National Provider Identifier, registered in NPPES, the national registry. Keep the name and address identical everywhere you use it.
  2. An Availity account. Create one as a healthcare provider. BlueCross takes applications and answers status questions through Availity, in its BlueCross BlueShield payer space.
  3. A complete CAQH profile. Your chiropractor fills in the CAQH ProView profile, attaches current documents, gives BlueCross access to it and attests that it is correct. The attestation must be no more than 90 days old when BlueCross gets access. One section of BlueCross’s manual says 120 days; plan on 90.
  4. Electronic payments. Set up electronic funds transfer, electronic claims and remittances through Payer Enrollment Services, the vendor BlueCross uses.

If your group is in BlueCare or TennCareSelect, you also need a TennCare Medicaid ID before you apply. Register with TennCare.

The application

In Availity, submit a Practitioner Enrollment Form for each chiropractor. Sign in to Availity, then open the BlueCross enrollment form. A new practice also submits a Group Enrollment Form. A chiropractor who joins a group that already has a BlueCross contract must join every network the group is in.

BlueCross takes your chiropractor’s credentialing details from CAQH and sends every credentialing letter to the credentialing contact listed there, whatever the form says. Check that contact before you apply.

What BlueCross checks

BlueCross verifies each item against the CAQH profile. These are its base rules, with the differences it lists for chiropractors.

ItemWhat BlueCross needs
LicenseA current, full, unrestricted chiropractic license in each state where you treat
MalpracticeAt least $1 million per occurrence and $3 million aggregate
Work historyThe last five years, with any gap over six months explained
Disclosure questionsAll 26 answered, with a written explanation for each “yes”
Call coverageAround the clock. For a chiropractor, an answering machine, cell phone, answering service or another chiropractor counts
Education and trainingListed in full; BlueCross uses it to set the specialty
AcupunctureOnly if you offer it: your state license must list acupuncture
Not neededA DEA certificate or hospital privileges

You can review what you submitted, ask for a correction within 30 days of BlueCross receiving the completed application, and ask about its status at any time.

BlueCross’s six steps

  1. Intake audit. BlueCross confirms it has everything and writes if it needs more. Answer within 30 days, or it can delay or close your application.
  2. Credentialing. BlueCross verifies your chiropractor and sends an acceptance or denial letter. The date on that letter is the credentialing date, not the day you join the network.
  3. Contracting. BlueCross approves or denies each network you asked for and sends the contracts through its Contract Management System. If a network is not open to you, BlueCross sends a denial notice.
  4. Enrollment audit. BlueCross checks that credentialing and contracts are complete.
  5. Provider enrollment. BlueCross adds you to each approved network and sends acceptance letters with effective dates. Your chiropractor is in network from those dates, not before.
  6. Ecommerce. BlueCross sets up your electronic payments, remittances and claims, and writes to confirm that claims and payments are ready.

Track the application in Availity under Provider Enrollment, Updates and Changes, then Track a Request. BlueCross has a step-by-step guide to tracking a request. Until the acceptance letter arrives, tell BlueCross patients they are out of network before the visit. The guide to checking coverage before the visit covers what to ask them.

Staying in network

  • Every 36 months. BlueCross recredentials each chiropractor. It writes three to six months ahead and pulls the CAQH profile again. If you do not respond, it can remove you from the network.
  • When BlueCross asks. It asks you to update or attest the CAQH profile to keep its directory current. Attest within 90 days, or your chiropractor drops out of the directory patients search. Network status and claims do not change.
  • Before a change. Report a new address, location or hours ahead of time: chiropractor details in CAQH, everything else through the BlueCross change form in Availity. BlueCross’s manual asks for notice within 30 days before a change.

For credentialing questions, BlueCross’s credentialing department answers at 1-800-924-7141.

How Claims Native fits

Credentialing and the contract are between your practice and BlueCross, and only your chiropractor can attest the CAQH profile. Once BlueCross has your chiropractor in network, we set up enrollment with the clearinghouse and BlueCross so claims and remittances can flow. That takes weeks, and the payer sets the pace; we chase it and tell you where it stands. Remittances can go to one clearinghouse at a time, so if another service bills for you today, we agree the switch date with you first. Your money keeps going to your bank. We never hold it.

For the general picture across payers, read payer enrollment for a chiropractic practice. For what BlueCross expects on each claim, read what a clean chiropractic claim needs.

What is real today
  • This guide follows BlueCross BlueShield of Tennessee’s Provider Administration Manual for its commercial and Medicare Advantage networks, effective October 1, 2026. BlueCross can change its rules; check the current manual before you apply.
  • BlueCare and TennCareSelect add their own steps. Facilities and other organizations apply through a BlueCross network representative instead.
  • Applying does not mean BlueCross will accept you. Nothing here is legal advice; have your attorney read the contract before you sign.
