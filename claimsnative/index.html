The lanes

Thirteen lanes. One claim at a time.

Each lane owns its jobs and writes what it did to one ledger. The next lane reads that ledger. Nothing hands off by memory.

  1. 1

    Intake

    Reads each visit from the practice system and keeps the source row behind every claim.

    Hands off
    A claim version with its lineage, ready for a coverage check.
    Stops for
    A visit row it cannot read, or a field the claim needs that the source never captured.

    Practice exportSource record

  2. 2

    Coverage

    Checks eligibility and benefits for the member, payer, and service date.

    Hands off
    A dated coverage answer with copay and deductible facts attached to the claim.
    Stops for
    A member id that does not resolve to one person at the payer.

    270/271

  3. 3

    Authorization

    Finds the lines a payer rule holds for prior authorization and drafts the request.

    Hands off
    A request the provider can send, with units and dates computed from the claim.
    Stops for
    The provider must sign the clinical justification; no agent writes one.

    Payer ruleAuthorization request

  4. 4

    Coding

    Reads every billed line against the signed note and cites the sentences it rests on.

    Hands off
    A cited support verdict per line for the provider to confirm.
    Stops for
    Every code verdict is a clinical act, so the provider decides.

    Signed noteCPT and ICD-10 lines

    Scrub and correction

    Runs the fixed 837P checks, explains each finding, and proposes a corrected claim version.

    Hands off

    Stops for
    A missing clinical fact, such as a diagnosis, that no stored record supplies.

    837P checksClaim version

    Release

    Binds a release intent to the exact claim version and routes it to the approvers the practice policy names.

    Hands off

    Stops for

    Release intentApproval

    Filing and acknowledgment

    Builds the 837P, files it through the clearinghouse, and records the 999 and 277CA that come back.

    Hands off
    A filed claim with its clearinghouse reference and every acknowledgment kept at its own level.
    Stops for
    A rejection whose fix needs a fact the record does not hold.

    837P999277CA

    Status follow-up

    Writes down the timely-filing deadline and asks the payer where each filed claim stands.

    Hands off
    A dated payer status per claim and a list of claims going stale.

    276/277

How it works

Agents do the work. You make the calls.

01 · Connect

Your visits, payers, and clearinghouse

Bring the visit export your practice already has. Enrollment with your payers and the clearinghouse starts at launch.

02 · Agents run every lane

Check, correct, file, follow up, post, match

Every job cites the record it acted on and writes to one ledger you can read.

03 · You decide

A short list, each item named

Only a legal act, a clinical judgment, a financial authority, or missing evidence reaches you.

Where people stay in charge

It stops for four reasons.

A legal act

Releasing a claim or signing an appeal. A named approver does it.

A clinical judgment

A diagnosis, a code verdict, or a medical-necessity answer. The provider decides.

A financial authority

A write-off, a refund, or a payment above the practice's own limit. The office decides.

Missing evidence

A fact the record does not hold. The office supplies it and the job runs again.

Everything else, the agents finish. A model may explain, classify, and draft. It never computes money, never declares a claim valid, and never says a payer accepted anything.

Questions

What does Claims Native do today?

It runs the billing department as agents: coverage checks, coding review against the signed note, scrub and correction, release routing, 837P filing with 999 and 277CA acknowledgments, status follow-up, denial classification and appeal drafts, 835 posting, bank deposit matching, patient balances, and a daily close. Every job cites its evidence. You can watch it run today on synthetic data.

Is electronic filing live?

The demo files to a test clearinghouse. In the pilot, filing runs in test mode until your payer and clearinghouse enrollment completes and you turn it on. The page always says which mode it is in.

Does Claims Native guarantee revenue or reimbursement?

No. The calculator is a planning estimate, not a revenue promise. The pilot does not guarantee submission, payer acceptance, reimbursement, revenue, or legal correctness.
