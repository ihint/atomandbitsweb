What we do

The billing work, from the signed visit to the deposit in your bank.

Here is the work Claims Native takes on. Every step leaves a record you can open, and we tell you plainly which steps we are still finishing.

  1. Visit to claim. We turn each signed visit into a claim, starting from a visit file you export from your EHR.
  2. Coverage. We check the patient’s insurance before a claim goes out, and the day before a scheduled visit when the last check is getting old.
  3. Scrub. We hold each claim up against the payer’s rules and fix what we can before it leaves.
  4. Filing. We send each claim to the payer through Stedi, a national clearinghouse.
  5. Follow-up. We watch every filing deadline and ask the payer about any claim that goes quiet.
  6. Payments. We read the payer’s payment file line by line and tie each payment to its claim.
  7. Patient balance. We work out what the patient owes from the payer’s payment and explanation.
  8. Deposit match. We match each bank deposit to the payment behind it.
  9. Posting. Posting payments back into your EHR is the step we are still proving. So far it has run on a test ledger we built, not on a practice’s EHR.

What you get back: a clear view of every claim, and a short list of the questions that need your office.

A claim comes to you when it needs your provider’s judgment or something your front desk knows, like a new insurance card.

How it fits your office

Keep the EHR your team already knows.

Your front desk finally has its routine down, and we want to leave it that way. We work from the system you have.

  • Today your visits reach us as a file you export from your EHR, and you look over each upload before we use it.
  • Claims go to payers through Stedi, the clearinghouse we connect to.
  • We are building connections that read visits straight from Jane and ChiroHD and post payments back. Neither is live yet.
  • Your money keeps going to your bank. We read deposits so we can match them, and we never hold or move your funds.

What stays with you

What stays in your hands.

  • Clinical judgment. Your provider decides what the signed note supports. We never add a clinical fact, and we leave any coding call that needs your provider to your provider.
  • Collecting patient balances. We tell you what each patient owes, and your front desk keeps that conversation with the patient.
  • Eligibility problems from intake. When the wrong card is on file, or a patient had no coverage that day, we flag it and hand it back to your office to sort out with the patient.

When a payment is short

When an EOB comes in lower than you expected.

You know the moment: you open the explanation of benefits and the number is smaller than it should be. We plan to compare each payment with your contract and the payer’s explanation, and to go back to the payer with a corrected claim, a reconsideration or an appeal when your contract supports more. Then we record what that payer needed, so the next claim goes out the way the payer wants it.

That comparison isn’t built yet. As of October 19, 2026, checking payments against your contract is still ahead of us, and so is any promise to recover the full contracted amount. We won’t offer either until it works.

Reconsiderations, appeals and payer disputes go out under the billing authorization your practice signs with us.

What we do

  • The night before each clinic day. We check insurance for the patients on the next day’s schedule, so your front desk starts the morning with one sheet showing whether coverage is active, the copay, the deductible left, visits used and left, and anyone who needs a call.
  • Every week. We ask each payer where your open claims stand. When one passes 30 days, we dig into why it is stuck and what will move it.
  • When a claim is denied or stuck. We hand your biller what needs fixing, along with a drafted corrected claim or appeal. Your biller or your office looks it over and sends it, and nothing goes to a payer without your OK.
  • Every Friday. A one-page money report: the money you’re expecting, the money that’s stuck, what we did that week, and the few items that need you.
