What we do

The billing work, from visit to payment.

Eight steps to review for your practice. Each needs an agreed scope and proof that it is ready.

  1. Visit to claim. We turn each signed visit in your EHR into a claim.
  2. Coverage. Eligibility checks must be ready before live filing.
  3. Scrub. We check each claim against the payer’s rules and fix what we can.
  4. Filing. Live filing starts only after enrollment, workflow checks and practice approval.
  5. Follow-up. We track every filing deadline and every claim that goes quiet.
  6. Posting. Payment posting into your EHR needs a tested return path and an agreed review process.
  7. Patient balance. We work out what the patient owes from the payer’s payment and explanation.
  8. Deposit match. Deposit matching needs both the remittance and the bank record.

What to expect: a record of each step in the agreed scope, with unresolved items kept open.

You only see a claim when it needs a provider’s decision or a fact only you have.

Your EHR

Keep your existing EHR.

What to check before choosing a connection

Nothing moves to a new system. We’ll confirm the connection approach during onboarding.

  • First review: a visit file. Files must match our supported layout. We review a recent export with patient details removed to confirm whether it fits. An export alone does not establish support for your EHR.
  • Review before access: a dedicated EHR login. We confirm whether a dedicated login is permitted and which tasks it can support. Naming Jane, ChiroHD or another EHR does not establish a live connection. EHR reading and posting must pass practice-specific checks before use.
  • New work for each system: a direct connection. A connection through an EHR’s own interface takes development for that EHR. None is live yet, and you do not need one to start.

Whichever approach fits, claims go out through the clearinghouse, and your money keeps going to your bank. We never hold it.

What stays with you

Three things we never take over.

  • Clinical judgment. Your provider decides what the signed note supports. We never add a clinical fact or make a coding call that needs the provider.
  • Collecting patient balances. We tell you what the patient owes. Collecting it stays with your office.
  • Eligibility problems from intake. Wrong insurance on file, or no coverage on the date of service: we report them, and you own them.

When a payment is short

We keep the difference open.

We check the difference against the payer’s explanation and your contract. When the contract supports more, we go back to the payer for it: a corrected claim, a reconsideration or an appeal. Then we record what the payer needed, so the next claim goes out the way that payer wants it.

A short payment does not become a patient bill by default.

A person reviews every reconsideration, appeal and payer dispute before it goes out.

Questions

What practices ask first.

Which specialties do you work with?

Practices across medical specialties that bill professional claims to insurers. Primary care, behavioral health, cardiology, dermatology, orthopedics and chiropractic are examples, not a full list. We do not bill hospital or facility claims. Tell us your specialty, and we look at your codes and payers during setup.

Where do you operate?

Nationwide. We work with practices across the U.S. from Chattanooga, Tennessee, and file claims through a national clearinghouse. That does not mean every payer is ready on day one. We add payers one at a time and confirm yours during setup. Secondary claims come later.

Do I have to change EHRs?

No. Keep your existing EHR. We’ll confirm the connection approach during onboarding. A visit file must match our supported layout; a dedicated EHR login needs a review of access, terms and the proposed tasks before use.

How does setup work?

It starts with how you bill today: your specialty, your payers, your EHR and who does your billing. Then you send one export of a recent week with patient details removed, you sign the agreement and the business associate agreement, and we enroll you with the clearinghouse and your payers. A shadow period on your own visits comes before live filing, and your office and your provider approve the switch. See each step.

What happens if my EHR isn’t listed?

Choose “Another EHR” when you set up your practice, or name it in an email to us. We start from a visit file and review your system with you. A direct connection to a new system is development work, and we tell you so before you rely on one.
