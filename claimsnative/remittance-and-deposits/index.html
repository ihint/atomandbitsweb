Does a remittance mean the money reached your bank?

No. A remittance explains a payer’s decision about claims. It does not, by itself, prove that the practice received money in its bank account. Check the remittance and the bank record together.

Match the explanation to the money

The payer may send the electronic remittance advice and the funds transfer through different channels. CMS describes reassociation: matching the payment to the remittance using the matching trace information. The files can arrive at different times. See CMS’s payment and remittance guide.

For a useful comparison, keep the payer, payment amount, payment date and trace information together in your secure billing records. Check the bank’s posted record. A pending transfer, a clearinghouse acknowledgment and an explanation of benefits each prove a different thing.

When the amounts differ

First check whether the bank payment covers more than one remittance or claim. Then review the payer’s adjustments and any offsets. A difference may need an explanation before anyone can decide whether the payer owes more or a patient owes anything. Do not turn an unexplained difference into a patient balance.

Keep an unmatched payment open with a named next action. Ask your billing service to show which amounts match, which need review and who owns the follow-up. Keep patient records, bank details and payment trace numbers out of inquiry forms and email.

Rejection, denial and receipt are different

A claim acknowledgment can report acceptance or rejection. That is not a payment. A later remittance explains the payer’s adjudication, including adjustments or denials. Stedi’s workflow guide shows those stages. Follow the actual response and the payer’s rules before choosing a correction or appeal.

What to check with Claims Native

Ask us how your practice will supply remittances and bank evidence, who reviews differences and where results will appear in your EHR. We confirm the connection approach during onboarding. A working remittance import does not establish a live bank connection or verified deposits; those depend on the practice’s payment setup and available records.

We agree the work and price before starting. Your provider keeps clinical authority, and your office keeps control of the bank relationship. Read the setup checks, then bring one billing problem to a conversation without patient or bank details.
