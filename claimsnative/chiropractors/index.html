Coding lane rules

Check what matters. Never invent what is missing.

CMT review

  • Compare the selected 98940, 98941, or 98942 code with the count of documented treated spinal regions.
  • Check that the diagnosis links and visit evidence the lane requires are present.
  • Stop when the code, documented regions, units, or modifier rule do not agree.

X-ray review

  • Compare the body area and view count on the service line with the order, report, and visit record.
  • Check the service date, diagnosis link, and same-day or frequency rule.
  • Stop when evidence is missing, contradictory, or outside the lane.

A stop names the mismatch and the person who resolves it. No agent selects a more payable code or fills a clinical gap.

Denials and money

The lanes after the claim leaves.

Denials

Classified by reason code

CARC 50 medical necessity stops for the provider. CARC 197 missing authorization goes to the office with the packet drafted.

Remittance

835 posted from the payer's figures

CO adjustments and PR patient share are kept apart, never netted.

Settlement

Deposit matched to the trace

A deposit that is short by a dollar is a question for the office, not a number to force.

Separate authority

Office approval is not provider approval.

Office approval

  • Patient and coverage fields.
  • Rendering provider, location, visit date, and charges.
  • Duplicates, missing fields, and office-policy exceptions.

Provider approval

  • Diagnosis selection and links to service lines.
  • CMT regions and code choice.
  • X-ray body area, views, and clinical support.

A material edit starts a new claim version and clears any approval that no longer applies.

Questions

Know where the agents stop.

Does Claims Native submit claims to a payer?

In the pilot, filing runs in test mode until your enrollment completes and you turn it on. Then the 837P goes to your payers and the acknowledgment and status come back onto the ledger.

What do the CMT and X-ray checks do?

They compare the code and the line against the signed note, the order, and the report, and stop for the provider on any mismatch. They never pick a code.

Why are office and provider approvals separate?

Office staff answer whether the administrative facts are right. The provider answers whether the claim matches the care. Both attach to the exact claim version.
