Fractional CPO/CTO

Where would a seasoned product and technology leader help you most?

You’re building a healthcare company that sells to health plans or providers. They ask a lot before they sign: integrations, security reviews, a roadmap they can trust. I’d love to own the product and technology decisions with you, so you get a seasoned leader without the full-time hire.

When it helps most

It helps most at one of three moments. A health plan or a health system asks for a security review, an integration or a roadmap before it will sign. A raise is coming or has just closed, and investors ask who owns product and technology. Or a first enterprise contract is in reach, and the product has to keep the promise the contract makes. I’ve been through each: SOC 2 and HIPAA work and a $12M Series A at TPN.health, and Paragon Truss’s first enterprise contract.

What draws me to this

I’m curious about the stretch between a health plan or a hospital saying yes and people using what you built. A member wants a clear answer. A nurse wants to reach the right doctor at 2 a.m. The buyer who signed wants to see that it worked. Each of them needs something different from your product, and working out what comes first is a puzzle I enjoy.

I’ve worked on both sides of that stretch. At PerfectServe I was the first product manager as hospitals brought the product to thousands of physicians. At BlueCross BlueShield of Tennessee I led digital products for 3.5 million members, with 18 direct reports and 130+ people across matrixed teams. I co-founded TheraMatch, and after TPN.health acquired it I served as acting CTO through a $12M Series A.

What it looks like

I work with you four to 20 days a month, take a seat in your leadership meetings, and own the product and technology decisions you hand me. I set the product direction, make the architecture calls, and still review the code. I love that mix.

Four days a month won’t run your engineering team for you. It is enough to set direction, make the product and technology calls with you, and tell you plainly when you need a full-time hire. Twenty days is full-time for a set period, for when you need someone in the seat every day.

The minimum is one month, and after that we go month to month.

The first 30 days

I start by meeting your team, your customers and the people closest to the job. By the end of the first month you have a written read of the product, the technology and the team: what’s working and what’s at risk. You have my recommendation on the one decision that matters most. And you have a 90-day plan with owners, cost and how we’ll judge it.

Price

You pay a fixed monthly fee for the days we agree, and we set it before we start.

If you’d rather start smaller, the Product & AI assessment is two weeks at a fixed fee.
