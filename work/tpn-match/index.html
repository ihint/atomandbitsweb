TPN Match · A question that changed the plan

We started by talking
about invoices.

Ian Harman · VP, Provider Networks & Technology · TPN.health · February 2025 to August 2026

When TheraMatch became part of TPN.health, we could do more than help someone find a provider. We could help that provider get paid for the care, too. One question about invoicing payers led us to an internal claims filing system and a platform to manage revenue capture.

What I asked

“What exactly are we trying to achieve here?”

Before deciding how to invoice payers, I wanted us to be clear about the result we were after.

Finding a shared purpose

First, we had to name the goal.

At TPN, we were discussing invoicing payers directly, and I asked what we were trying to achieve. Were we trying to improve throughput? Invoicing? Payouts?

Working through the answer together, we saw that TPN needed an internal claims filing system and a platform to manage revenue capture. With the goal named, we could see the product it called for.

That product would serve several people at once. Providers wanted clear referrals and predictable payment. Payer and TPA partners wanted accurate terms and reliable transactions. Care navigators wanted to know what was happening to the person seeking care.

Between a referral and a paid claim sat provider participation, payer terms, eligibility, healthcare EDI, claim repricing, remittance, and payment. We set out to build one product that served everyone along that path.

Growing TPN Match

Build for the company we wanted to become.

I led product and platform strategy as TPN Match expanded from matching into network, claims, and payment operations. My role also covered technical sales, integrations, and technology relationships with payer and TPA partners.

Members’ health information ran through every claim, so I also led SOC 2 security and privacy audit work, HIPAA compliance, and the HIPAA attestation milestone.

We put a complete payment path first and grew the roadmap from there. We learned from live claims while we built, and they showed us what would help TPN now and what could wait until that foundation was in place.

Choosing the tools

AI had a useful role. So did fixed rules and human judgment.

A knowledge graph organized claims and claim types from different sources. A machine-learning model used that information to estimate negotiated rates and reached production during my tenure. Contracted claim repricing used fixed rules based on CPT code, location, and effective terms.

AI also handled early inbound messages and gathered assessment details, so a navigator began each review with those details in hand. People kept the care and operating decisions that called for their judgment.

We gave each transaction state its own record, so the people responsible for a claim could see what had happened to it and what still needed attention. A submitted claim, an accepted claim, a remittance, and a payment each tell you something different. So do denials and failures.

We wanted TPN’s team to welcome more claims and conversations without adding staff at the same rate. Internal tools helped parse claims, build the knowledge base, support negotiated-rate learning, and prepare inbound conversations.

What the team delivered

A growing network and a live claims system.

TheraMatch and TPN Match reached $1M+ ARR and 1M members in under 12 months.

From January through June 2026, the product processed more than $1 million in claims volume. Our contracted base covered 120 TPAs and about 1.5 million eligible lives.

Across about 4,000 claims from April through June 2026, 99.59% passed repricing on the first attempt. Our negotiated-rate model reached production, and matching, claims, and payment ran together at TPN.

A team earned those results. I helped define what we needed to build, led the product and technology choices, and tied those choices to the payer relationships and operations that made the product useful to providers and partners.

Why I remember it

A better understanding changed the product.

That conversation is one of my favorite examples of a question changing the plan. I love that turn, when someone supplies a missing detail and a team suddenly sees a more useful thing to build. At TPN, naming the goal gave our product, commercial, and technical decisions one shared purpose.

That experience is why I’m building Claims Native. It brings what I learned at TPN, from claim preparation and checks to exceptions and payment follow-up, to independent practices, so the people who give the care get paid for it. It is in early access.
