Atom & Bits / Ian Harman / BlueCross BlueShield of Tennessee

Case study · Enterprise scale · Payer systems

Optimizing self service.

Ian Harman · Senior / Group Digital Product Manager · BlueCross BlueShield of Tennessee · April 2016 to October 2023

When members can find an answer and take the next step themselves, the service becomes easier to use and less costly to provide. At BlueCross BlueShield of Tennessee, I led digital products serving about 3.5 million members. Our opportunity reached across member, broker, employer, administrator, provider, and sales experiences, with team, investment, and platform decisions to match.

What changed

A service people could find and use.

Helping members use their benefits

One health plan had many users, channels, systems, and definitions of success.

A member wanted a clear answer. A broker needed a product that was easy to explain and quote. An employer needed a benefit it could manage. Sales, service, compliance, operations, and technology each carried part of the result.

What members needed

People could not use a health plan well if its information, navigation, and tools felt fragmented. Search, content, personalization, mobile, chat, and self-service needed to work as one experience.

What BlueCross needed

Each local feature touched shared data, platforms, processes, budgets, and vendors. We needed one product direction that could serve many audiences without turning every request into a separate system.

Choosing where to invest

Give the customer experience a clear direction.

01 / Portfolio

Led the digital experience

I led product decisions across web and mobile for members, brokers, employers, administrators, providers, and sales.

02 / Organization

Built alignment at scale

I managed 22 direct reports and led through a separate matrix of more than 130 people across product, design, technology, operations, and business teams.

03 / Investment

Connected money to product choices

I owned and deployed a $10 million member modernization budget and influenced about $60 million in broker, quoting, platform, Salesforce, and related investment.

Decisions that mattered

Build shared products without losing the needs of each audience.

Platform before page

Treat content, search, identity, data, and personalization as shared capabilities.

We moved beyond one-off channel work and made platform choices that could support web, mobile, service, broker, and employer experiences together.

Usefulness before novelty

Make personalization change the next action.

Personalization helped members find and use benefits. It also became part of the value story for large self-funded employer groups.

Adoption before launch

Measure whether people changed behavior.

We tracked engagement, app adoption, self-service, member satisfaction, sales support, and operating savings to understand whether the investment changed how people used the service.

Influence with clear ownership

Be clear about the investment I could direct.

I owned the $10 million member modernization budget and influenced about $60 million in broader broker, quoting, and platform programs.

What changed

Digital products served about 3.5 million members and helped BlueCross grow.

Average NPS stayed above 60 across the multi-year period. Personalization increased web engagement 15% and mobile engagement 12%. Digital adoption more than doubled.

Member and business value

Member replatform and self-service improvements supported tens of millions of dollars in annual sales and more than $500,000 in administrative savings. Personalization became a selling point for large self-funded groups.

New channels

Connected-device strategy increased engagement 50% and supported millions of dollars in annual sales. These results connected product adoption to a clear business case.

What this means for a business

A better customer experience can change the cost of serving it.

I ask founders where customers still rely on people to do something a product could make easier. At BlueCross, improving that experience increased adoption, reduced administrative cost, and gave BlueCross more to offer employer groups. Product choices, the team, and the investment had to support the same result.

Evidence and limits

These are my reported results, delivered by broad teams under my product leadership. I owned the $10 million budget; the $60 million figure covers programs I influenced. This page is independent of BlueCross BlueShield of Tennessee.
