Atom & Bits / Ian Harman / BlueCross BlueShield of Tennessee

Case study · Enterprise scale · Payer systems

Members finding their own answers.

Ian Harman · Senior / Group Digital Product Manager · BlueCross BlueShield of Tennessee · April 2016 to October 2023

At BlueCross BlueShield of Tennessee, I led digital products serving about 3.5 million members. When a member can find an answer and take the next step on their own, they know where to go, and the health plan is less costly to serve. Our opportunity reached across member, broker, employer, administrator, provider and sales experiences, with team, investment and platform decisions to match.

Helping members use their benefits

One health plan served many people, and each wanted something different.

A member wanted a clear answer. A broker wanted a product that was easy to explain and quote. An employer wanted a benefit it could manage. Sales, service, compliance, operations and technology teams each carried part of the result.

What members wanted

Members wanted to search, read, ask a question and take care of things themselves, on the web or on their phone. We set out to make search, content, personalization, mobile, chat and self-service work as one experience.

What BlueCross wanted

Every feature a member or broker saw drew on shared data, platforms, processes, budgets and vendors. BlueCross wanted one product direction that could serve all those audiences, so each new request could build on what was already there.

Choosing where to invest

Room to build for every audience.

01 / Portfolio

Led web and mobile for every audience

I led product decisions across web and mobile for members, brokers, employers, administrators, providers and sales.

02 / Organization

Built and led the team

I managed 22 direct reports and led through a separate matrix of more than 130 people across product, design, technology, operations and business teams. The results on this page belong to them too.

03 / Investment

Invested in members and brokers

I owned and deployed a $10 million member modernization budget and influenced about $60 million in broker, quoting, platform, Salesforce and related investment.

Decisions that mattered

Build shared products that give each audience what it came for.

Platform before page

Treat content, search, identity, data and personalization as shared capabilities.

We chose platforms that web, mobile, service, broker and employer experiences could share, so an improvement in one place could reach every audience.

Usefulness before novelty

Make personalization change the next action.

Personalization helped members find and use their benefits. It also became part of what BlueCross offered large self-funded employer groups.

Adoption before launch

Measure whether people changed behavior.

I wanted to know whether members did anything differently. We tracked engagement, app adoption, self-service, member satisfaction, sales support and operating savings to find out.

Influence with clear ownership

Be clear about the investment I could direct.

I owned the $10 million member modernization budget and influenced about $60 million in broader broker, quoting and platform programs.

What changed

Digital adoption more than doubled, and BlueCross grew with it.

Our products served about 3.5 million members, and average NPS stayed above 60 across the multi-year period. Personalization increased web engagement 15% and mobile engagement 12%.

Value for members and BlueCross

Member replatform and self-service improvements supported tens of millions of dollars in annual sales and more than $500,000 in administrative savings. Personalization became a selling point for large self-funded groups.

New channels

Connected-device strategy increased engagement 50% and supported millions of dollars in annual sales. Member engagement and sales grew together.

What this means for founders

A better customer experience can change the cost of serving it.

I like to ask founders what their customers would gladly do on their own if a product made it easy. At BlueCross, making that easier increased adoption, reduced administrative cost and gave BlueCross more to offer employer groups. Product choices, the team and the investment all served the same result.

Evidence and limits

These are my reported results, delivered by broad teams under my product leadership. I owned the $10 million budget; the $60 million figure covers programs I influenced. This page is independent of BlueCross BlueShield of Tennessee.
