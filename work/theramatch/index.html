Atom & Bits / Ian Harman / TheraMatch

Case study · Zero to one · Acquisition

I built TheraMatch from the first idea through acquisition.

Ian Harman · Co-Founder · TheraMatch

TheraMatch started with a simple question: why do people choose a therapist from a list when fit matters so much? Ian co-founded the company, built the product and technical stack, helped sell it, and led its move into TPN.health after the acquisition.

What changed

A thesis became a business and an acquired product.

The problem

A directory could show who was available. It could not show who might fit.

People seeking care often had to choose from short profiles, broad specialty labels, and a few filters. Practices had the same problem when they referred patients. A poor first match cost time, trust, and momentum at the moment someone had decided to seek help.

The unmet need

The product needed to turn clinical frameworks, patient preferences, provider traits, and real availability into a useful first connection. It also needed to stay humble. Software could improve the choice. It could not promise a clinical outcome or replace the relationship.

The business need

TheraMatch needed more than an algorithm. It needed a clear buyer, a repeatable intake flow, provider participation, measurable proof, and a path from referral to retained care.

What Ian owned

Product, code, customer work, and the path into a larger company.

01 / Product

Turned clinical ideas into a usable flow

Designed the assessment, data capture, provider model, matching logic, forms, and automated services around more than 20 psychographic measures and clinical frameworks.

02 / Technology

Built the full stack

Built the website, intake, application flows, data model, matching system, and operating tools with Elixir, Phoenix, LiveView, and Ecto.

03 / Business

Helped create the commercial system

Worked with physician practices, providers, and partners. Helped shape contracts, adoption, proof, and the story that carried the product through acquisition.

The hard decisions

The value lived in the choices around the algorithm.

Measure care, not clicks

Define a successful match as a retained first connection.

TheraMatch did not count a recommendation as success. The core measure asked whether the person was still with the first therapist at the one-month follow-up.

Use structure without false certainty

Let software improve the choice without claiming to know the relationship.

The system used structured measures and provider information. It did not treat a score as a diagnosis or a guarantee.

Build lean

Prove the product before raising institutional capital.

The team built, sold, and operated the product without institutional funding before the transaction.

Integrate after the deal

Turn a point product into a platform capability.

After the acquisition, Ian translated the product strategy and technical direction for a broader team and helped integrate matching into TPN's provider, navigation, claims, and payment work.

What changed

TheraMatch moved from an idea to a product people used, paid for, and acquired.

Twelve physician clients across three practices referred patients into the product. About 40 providers took part. About 300 matches formed the basis of the 98% one-month first-match retention result. The business reached about $12,000 in monthly recurring revenue before the acquisition.

The transaction

TPN.health announced the acquisition of All Counseling and TheraMatch on February 27, 2025. The deal gave TheraMatch access to a much larger provider base and gave TPN a matching product it could connect to network and care operations.

The next chapter

The acquisition changed the scale of the problem. The product no longer ended at a match. It had to connect to provider participation, navigation, eligibility, claims, and payment.

What the work proves

Ian can find a real need, build the product, sell the idea, and carry it into scale.

The case joins clinical reasoning, product judgment, hands-on development, customer work, commercial proof, and post-deal integration. That range is the point.

Evidence and limits

The acquisition is public. Product, customer, revenue, provider, and retention figures are Ian’s account of TheraMatch’s work. The 98% result means the person remained with the first therapist at the one-month follow-up. It does not claim a clinical outcome.
