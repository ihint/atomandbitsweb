Atom & Bits / TheraMatch

TheraMatch

A list of names can leave the hardest part to the person asking for help.

Ian Harman · Co-Founder · August 2024–April 2025

A directory can narrow the search for a therapist by location, specialty, or insurance. A person still has to decide whom they might feel able to work with. TheraMatch began with that question of fit.

The problem

Help someone take the next step.

When someone decides to seek help, giving them more names does not necessarily make the next step easier. The information needs to help them make a useful first connection. That was the problem I worked on as a co-founder of TheraMatch.

I built the product, technology, and strategy, including the initial matching algorithm and clinical delivery framework for our early clients. The work brought clinical frameworks, patient preferences, and provider information into the product’s matching logic.

There were people on both sides of that match. Physician practices needed a useful referral path. Therapists needed to participate. The person seeking care needed a connection they could begin with. We had to build the product and the work around it together.

Product decisions

Follow the connection beyond the recommendation.

The product used more than 20 psychographic measures and clinical frameworks. I built the application with Elixir, Phoenix, LiveView, and Ecto, including the intake, matching logic, data model, and operating tools.

The important question was what happened after a recommendation. The measure we used asked whether a person was still with the first therapist at the one-month follow-up. That gave us a way to learn about the connection beyond a click or an introduction.

A matching score could help inform a choice. It could not guarantee a therapeutic relationship or a clinical outcome. The product had to remain useful within that limit.

Customers and acquisition

Build something people can use and a business that can support it.

We won 12 physician clients across three practices. About 40 providers took part, and about 300 matches formed the basis of the reported 98% one-month first-match retention result. The business reached about $12,000 in monthly recurring revenue before acquisition.

Typical contract value was $4,000–$5,000 from September 2024 to January 2025. Building the product also meant working with customers on the terms and day-to-day use that would make it a business.

TPN.health announced its acquisition of All Counseling and the TheraMatch technology on February 27, 2025. I led the acquisition and integration work. The matching product then became part of a broader provider-access business, with new responsibilities around networks, navigation, claims, and payment.
