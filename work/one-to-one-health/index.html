Atom & Bits / One to One Health

One to One Health

Growing a business around care people value.

Ian Harman · VP, Product & Engineering; Chief Privacy Officer · October 2023–June 2024

When demand grows, you still have to keep the promise that brought customers to you. At One to One Health, that meant planning for the full care experience while giving clinicians and operations staff something useful sooner.

Growing One to One Health

When people want more of what your business offers, you ought to have reason to celebrate. It is harder to enjoy that growth when you can see how much more it will ask of the people who made it possible.

When I joined One to One Health, patients valued the care and clinicians enjoyed delivering it. We had an opportunity to bring that care to more people. But we still relied on manual processes, spreadsheets, and knowledge held by a few people. We needed to grow without asking those people to carry every new client through by hand.

There was a less obvious problem, too. We could staff for a new client and still wait for that client’s patients to begin using the care. Eligibility and onboarding delays stood between the capacity we had paid for and the people it was meant to serve. Adding clinicians could not, by itself, resolve that delay. We had to understand what prevented the service from reaching people in the first place.

I set out a product plan that connected eligibility and onboarding to the care itself. It gave us a direction for the full product suite, but the clinical and operations teams needed to see value sooner than that roadmap could deliver it.

That is why I started with clinical documentation. I designed and developed the initial AI infrastructure to prepare clinical notes for provider review. Charting time fell from 22 to 17.5 minutes across all encounters, while clinician and patient satisfaction were maintained or improved. We could give the team something useful sooner while building toward the larger plan. I also hired the founding engineers who would help us build the wider product suite.

As a founder, I have to make room for a larger ambition while meeting immediate needs. I want to find something we can do now that helps customers and staff, while bringing that ambition closer. That is how I think about an engagement.

Choosing where to invest

Giving the product plan a team and a budget.

Our wider plan needed an engineering team, investment, and care with the systems that held patient information. I hired the founding engineers and held Chief Privacy Officer responsibility. I also negotiated a 17% reduction in IT spending.

Clinical and engineering colleagues helped shape the product and achieve these results. I had to connect what One to One Health needed next with what we could build, the people we needed to hire, and the resources available to us.

About this account and these results

I draw this account from my November 2023 product manifesto. Charting, satisfaction, hiring, and spending figures are my reported career results. My manifesto includes proposed work; this account does not claim that every part of that plan shipped or that the note workflow resolved the separate onboarding delays. Charting time is not total patient contact time.
