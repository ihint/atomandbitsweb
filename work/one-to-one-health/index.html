Building technology that scales access without losing the relationship.

The work

Product had to support care, operations, and trust at the same time.

One to One Health delivers relationship-based primary care through onsite and on-demand models. The technology work had to expand access without turning continuity into a transaction.

The operating challenge

Clinical workflows carry information, judgment, responsibility, follow-up, and patient trust. Adding technology can reduce administrative burden, but it can also fragment context or create a new queue for clinicians.

The product function therefore had to own the whole interaction between the care model, clinical team, member experience, employer customer, platform, and vendor ecosystem.

The contribution

  • Established product and engineering leadership
  • Translated the care model into digital capabilities
  • Connected privacy decisions to product and vendor design
  • Used AI inside a bounded clinical workflow with a measured operating outcome

The 20% figure is an operating result from Ian's work at One to One Health. It describes clinician time per encounter, not a clinical-outcome or reimbursement claim. Proprietary implementation details are intentionally omitted.

What the experience taught

Humans deliver care. Technology should create capacity around them.

01

Start with clinical work

Observe the information, judgment, follow-up, and exception handling before asking a model to automate anything.

02

Protect accountable authority

The system can prepare and route work while the appropriate clinician retains consequential judgment.

03

Measure operating change

Usage is not enough. Track whether the workflow reduces time, latency, error, or downstream burden without weakening trust.

How it applies now

AI becomes useful when it disappears into a better operating loop.

This experience informs Atom & Bits work on healthcare AI operations: begin with a repeated burden, bound the system's authority, preserve the evidence, and measure what changed for the accountable person doing the work.

It also informs Claims Native, where software prepares and checks claims while the office and provider retain separate approval authority.
