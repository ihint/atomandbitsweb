One to One Health

Scaling care delivery

Ian Harman · VP, Product & Engineering; Chief Privacy Officer · October 2023–June 2024

Patients valued the care at One to One Health, and more clients wanted it. I planned the full product suite for that growth and started with something clinicians could use early: AI-prepared notes that gave back an average of 4.5 minutes of charting per encounter.

Growing One to One Health

When I joined One to One Health, clinicians enjoyed delivering the care, and every new client was good news. Each one also asked more of a team that ran on manual processes, spreadsheets, and knowledge held by a few people. I wanted growth to stay good news for that team, too.

I also saw a less obvious opportunity: helping a new client’s patients start care sooner. We could staff for a new client and still wait for that client’s patients to begin using the care. Eligibility and onboarding delays stood between the capacity we had paid for and the people it was meant to serve. Adding clinicians could not, by itself, resolve that delay.

So I put eligibility and onboarding in our product plan alongside clinical care, set that direction for the full product suite, and hired the founding engineers. Building that suite would take time, and clinical and operations staff needed something useful while we built it.

I chose clinical documentation as a place to start. I designed and developed the initial AI infrastructure to prepare clinical notes for provider review. Charting time fell from 22 to 17.5 minutes across all encounters, giving clinicians back an average of 4.5 minutes per encounter, while clinician and patient satisfaction were maintained or improved.

As a founder, I like to think big and work small. An early improvement should help customers or staff today and bring the larger ambition closer. That is still how I choose where to begin an engagement.

Choosing where to invest

Giving the product plan a team and a budget.

Our wider plan called for an engineering team, investment, and care with patient information. I hired the founding engineers and, as Chief Privacy Officer, was responsible for the systems that stored that information, so patients could trust us with it. I also negotiated a 17% reduction in IT spending.

Clinical and engineering colleagues shaped the product and achieved these results with me. My part was to match what One to One Health wanted next with what we could build, the people to hire, and the budget we had.

About this account and these results

I draw this account from my November 2023 product manifesto, and the figures are my reported career results. Charting time is not total patient contact time. My manifesto set out a wider operating plan; this account does not claim that all of it shipped or that the note workflow resolved the separate onboarding delays.
