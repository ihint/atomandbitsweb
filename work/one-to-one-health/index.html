Atom & Bits / One to One Health

One to One Health

Growing the service people came to us for.

Ian Harman · VP, Product & Engineering; Chief Privacy Officer · October 2023–June 2024

A care business can have demand, a committed team, and still find growth difficult to absorb. This is an account of the operating questions and product work I took on at One to One Health.

The story

When people want more of what your business offers, you ought to have reason to celebrate. It is harder to enjoy that growth when you can see how much more it will ask of the people who made it possible.

At One to One Health, we had a care model that patients valued and clinicians enjoyed delivering. We also had important parts of the business running through manual work, spreadsheets, and knowledge held by a few people. Growing the service meant paying close attention to what we were asking those people to do.

There was a less obvious problem, too. We could staff for a new client and still wait for that client’s patients to begin using the care. Eligibility and onboarding delays stood between the capacity we had paid for and the people it was meant to serve. Adding clinicians could not, by itself, resolve that delay. We had to understand what prevented the service from reaching people in the first place.

My product plan connected eligibility and onboarding with the work of the care team. We needed to consider the whole service when deciding where technology could help.

Clinical documentation presented a different opportunity. I designed and developed the initial AI infrastructure to help prepare clinical notes for provider review. Charting time fell from 22 to 17.5 minutes across all encounters, while clinician and patient satisfaction were maintained or improved. I also hired the founding engineers who would help us build the product.

What interests me about that result is what we were trying to preserve. People wanted the care we offered. The work was to make more of it possible without allowing the effort of running the service to consume the time needed to deliver it. There is a great deal to enjoy in building a business when growth gives good people more room to do the work they care about.

The wider responsibility

Building the team behind the product.

The product work also called for an engineering team and care with the systems that held patient information. I hired the founding engineers and held Chief Privacy Officer responsibility. I also negotiated a 17% reduction in IT spending.

The clinical and engineering teams contributed to the work and its results. My responsibility was to help define what we needed to build and bring together the people and technology to deliver it.

About this account and these results

The operating account draws on my November 2023 product manifesto. The charting, satisfaction, hiring, and spending figures are my reported career results. The manifesto includes proposed work; this account does not claim that every part of that plan shipped or that the note workflow resolved the separate onboarding delays. Charting time is not total patient contact time.
